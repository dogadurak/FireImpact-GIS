About this project

Why Manavgat, and why this way

On July 28, 2021, a wildfire broke out near Manavgat, on Turkey's Mediterranean coast in Antalya province, and burned for ten days — the largest forest fire in the country's recorded history. This project was built for my application to the Esri Türkiye Campus Ambassador program, to demonstrate an end-to-end geospatial workflow: from raw Sentinel-2 satellite imagery to a validated risk model, entirely inside ArcGIS Pro.

The goal was not to produce the most visually impressive maps, but the most defensible ones. Before building a future fire-risk model, every assumption that would normally go into one — terrain aspect, slope, vegetation density — was tested against the real 2021 burn data using zonal statistics. One assumption held up under repeated testing; one did not, and was left out. That process is documented in full below.

02 · Scale of the Event

Ten days, one province, one record

The fire burned for ten days and became the largest forest fire in the history of the Turkish Republic. This analysis maps its physical extent, the meteorological conditions that let it spread, its cost in human exposure and carbon emissions, three years of vegetation recovery, and a forward-looking risk model — each built from satellite data and cross-checked against independent sources rather than produced in isolation.

The burned-area figure (60,128 ha) was not taken directly from a classifier's output. The raw dNBR classification initially reported 103,447 hectares — well above the 56,663–75,000 ha range reported by an independent satellite study and the Antalya Regional Forestry Directorate. Investigation showed most of the excess was scattered false-positive pixels across Antalya's urban core and farmland (cloud shadow, harvest signal), not fire. Keeping only contiguous burned patches over 100 hectares brought the total to 60,128 ha — within the independently reported range.

03 · Burn Severity (dNBR)

Measuring destruction: from yellow to deep maroon

Burn severity was measured with the differenced Normalized Burn Ratio (dNBR), computed from Sentinel-2's near-infrared (B08) and shortwave-infrared (B12) bands, then classified against standard USGS severity thresholds.

04 · Meteorological Context

An abnormally hot, typically dry Mediterranean summer

Against the 2015–2020 baseline, the real anomaly was temperature (+1.8°C above average) — the rainfall difference (-0.8 mm) was statistically negligible. This is deliberately not called a "drought": the data doesn't support that word, so it isn't used. The dominant wind during the fire blew from the north, accelerating its spread toward the coast.

05 · Model Validation

Testing assumptions before trusting them

Before building a future fire-risk model, common wildfire-science assumptions were tested against the real 2021 burn data using zonal statistics in ArcGIS Pro — not assumed, checked.

H1

South-facing slopes burn more severely

Mean real dNBR: south 0.436 · east/west 0.409 · north 0.364 — a clean, expected gradient.

H2

Denser pre-fire vegetation (higher NDVI) carries more fuel

Pre-fire NDVI rose steadily with severity: unburned areas averaged 0.42, the highest-severity class 0.70.

Confirmed
Aspect and pre-fire vegetation density both held up under repeated testing — including a re-check after removing classification noise, and again after correcting the terrain model's projection order — so both went into the final risk model in Section 9.

07 · Carbon Footprint

An estimated 2.97 million tonnes of CO₂

Calculated with the IPCC's 2006 AFOLU method (area × biomass × combustion factor × emission factor). Biomass was not assumed from a generic literature range — it was measured for this specific forest from ESA CCI Biomass's 2020 satellite product.

Estimated, not measured: burned area and biomass are real satellite data, but combustion completeness is still a literature-based assumption — hence the 2.38M–3.56M tonne range rather than one falsely precise number.
08 · 2022 → 2024

Nature's return from the ashes

Vegetation health (NBR) was tracked for three years, all symbolized on the same colour scale (-1 to 0.7) — using each year's own auto-stretched range would have let symbology differences masquerade as real recovery.

09 · Future Fire Risk Model

Built only from what held up

Based on Section 5's validation, the model combines only aspect (50%) and vegetation density / fuel load (50%) — the two variables confirmed against real 2021 burn data. It covers the whole landscape, not just the historical burn scar, so it also scores risk in forest that hasn't burned yet.

Conclusion

The validation is itself the finding

The 2021 Manavgat fire is a clear illustration of how climate extremes translate into ecological damage. In this project, every figure — burned area, meteorological anomaly, risk factors, human impact, carbon output — was either measured directly from satellite data or checked against an independent source before being reported.

The value of spatial intelligence in pre-disaster risk management doesn't come from a map that looks convincing. It comes from knowing which assumption survived contact with real data, and being willing to drop the one that didn't.
