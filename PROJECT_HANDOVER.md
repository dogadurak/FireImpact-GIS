# Manavgat 2021 Fire Impact and Recovery Analysis Project

## 1. Project Overview
This project focuses on the geospatial analysis of the devastating 2021 Manavgat wildfires in Turkey. The main goals are to measure the initial fire severity (burn severity) and track the vegetation recovery over the subsequent years (2022, 2023, 2024).

The core area of interest (AOI) lies across two Sentinel-2 tiles:
- **T36SUG** (Northern part of the fire)
- **T36SUF** (Southern part of the fire)

We specifically constrained our analysis to the confirmed fire extent to reduce noise and ensure high scientific accuracy.

## 2. Methodology and Indexes Used
- **NBR (Normalized Burn Ratio):** Calculated using Sentinel-2 bands B08 (NIR) and B12 (SWIR). `NBR = (B08 - B12) / (B08 + B12)`.
- **dNBR (Differenced Normalized Burn Ratio):** Calculated to assess burn severity by subtracting post-fire NBR from pre-fire NBR. `(dNBR = Pre_NBR - Post_NBR)`.
- **NDMI / Recovery NBR:** Used to track post-fire vegetation moisture and recovery over the years (2022, 2023, 2024) across the exact area burned in 2021.
- **TCI (True Color Image):** Extracted using RGB bands (B04, B03, B02) for visual reference and basemap context in the StoryMap.

## 3. Tech Stack and Libraries
The project heavily utilizes Python automation integrated with Esri's ArcGIS ecosystem:
- **ArcGIS Pro (arcpy):** Used for advanced geoprocessing, raster math, reclassification, mosaicking, and masking. Specifically, `arcpy.ia.RasterCalculator`, `arcpy.management.MosaicToNewRaster`, and `arcpy.management.ExtractByMask`.
- **Planetary Computer / pystac_client:** Used for programmatic searching and fetching of Sentinel-2 Level-2A imagery metadata and asset links from Microsoft's Planetary Computer STAC API.
- **rasterio & requests:** Used for downloading specific bands via signed URLs.
- **numpy:** For potential array manipulations (though `arcpy` was favored for final outputs due to handling locks and spatial references natively within the ArcGIS environment).

## 4. Key Datasets and Final Outputs
The following files are the finalized, scientifically accurate raster outputs generated by the project, stored in the `data/Sentinel2_Raw/` directory.

### Visual Basemaps (True Color)
- **`pre_tci_mosaic.tif`**: The pre-fire (July 2021) true-color image, seamlessly mosaicked from the North and South tiles. Used as the "before" basemap.
- **`post_tci_mosaic.tif`**: The post-fire (August 2021) true-color image. Used as the "after" basemap to visually demonstrate the immediate devastation.

### Fire Severity (2021)
- **`dNBR_Scientific.tif`**: The continuous decimal raster of dNBR values. Unclassified raw scientific data.
- **`dNBR_Classified.tif`**: The classified dNBR raster categorizing the burn into severity classes (Unburned, Low, Moderate-Low, Moderate-High, High).
- **`dNBR_Full_Masked_Classified.tif`**: The classified fire severity map. **Correction (see section 4b below): this raster is NOT clean** — it contains ~540,000 disconnected false-positive patches scattered across the whole AOI (urban Antalya, roads, farmland), on top of the real fire scars. Use `dNBR_True_Fire_Only.tif` (section 4b) for any area/severity statistic that needs to be accurate, not this file directly.

### Vegetation Recovery (2022 - 2024)
- **`Recovery_Full_NBR_2022.tif`**: The NBR index for the year 2022. Masked exactly to the `dNBR_Full_Masked_Classified.tif` extent. Demonstrates first-year recovery.
- **`Recovery_Full_NBR_2023.tif`**: The NBR index for the year 2023. Masked exactly to the burned area.
- **`Recovery_Full_NBR_2024.tif`**: The NBR index for the year 2024. Masked exactly to the burned area.

### Advanced Analysis
- **`Future_Fire_Risk_V4.tif`** (current, authoritative version): A predictive risk map combining **aspect** (slope orientation) and **pre-fire NDVI** (vegetation density/fuel load), each weighted 50%. Covers the full Sentinel-2 tile extent (not just the historical burn scar), so it also scores risk in surrounding forest that has not yet burned.
  - `Future_Fire_Risk.tif` (slope+aspect), `Future_Fire_Risk_V2.tif` (slope+aspect+2024 recovery-NBR proxy, burn-scar only), and `Future_Fire_Risk_V3.tif` (aspect+NDVI, but aspect computed on the DEM *before* reprojecting to UTM) are earlier iterations, kept for history but superseded by V4.
  - **Why slope was dropped:** `scripts/21_validate_risk_with_dnbr.py` tested the model's assumptions against the real 2021 dNBR data via zonal statistics. Aspect was confirmed (south-facing slopes burned more severely: mean dNBR 0.0225 vs. 0.0122 north-facing). Slope was **not** confirmed — flatter terrain actually burned more severely than steep terrain in this fire (likely denser/more continuous fuel in valley-bottom areas). Re-tested twice more for rigor: (1) against the true (noise-removed) fire mask in `scripts/28_revalidate_with_true_fire_mask.py` — still not confirmed (non-monotonic: mid-slope > steep > flat); (2) with slope computed the methodologically correct way (project DEM to UTM *first*, then compute `Slope()` on the projected DEM, rather than computing on the geographic DEM and reprojecting the result) in an ad-hoc verification — still not confirmed, nearly identical numbers. Conclusion is robust to both data-quality and methodology concerns; slope stays out.
  - **Why NDVI was added:** `scripts/23_download_ndvi_bands.py` + `scripts/24_ndvi_fuel_analysis.py` computed pre-fire (July 2021) NDVI and tested it against real burn severity classes. Result is a clean, monotonic relationship: Unburned areas averaged NDVI 0.42, rising steadily to 0.70 in the highest-severity class — denser/healthier pre-fire vegetation carried more fuel and burned more severely. See `manavgat_ndvi_siddet_iliskisi.png`.
  - **V3 → V4 correction:** V3's aspect was computed on the DEM while still in geographic (degree-based) coordinates, then the *result* was reprojected to UTM — methodologically backwards (should project the DEM first, then compute aspect on the projected/metric surface). V4 (`scripts/30_correct_terrain_and_risk_v4.py`) redoes this in the correct order. Verification: V3 and V4 agree on classification for **96.43%** of pixels — the fix was good practice, not a bug fix; conclusions are unchanged.
  - Build/rebuild with `scripts/30_correct_terrain_and_risk_v4.py` (requires `scripts/25_update_risk_model_v3.py` to have been run first, for `Pre_Fire_NDVI_Full.tif`).

### 4b. Critical Data-Quality Finding: Classification Noise, and the True Fire Mask
`dNBR_Full_Masked_Classified.tif` classifies ~103,447 ha as "burned" — but independent sources report the real Manavgat fire at 56,663 ha (satellite study) to 75,000 ha (Antalya Forestry Directorate). Investigation (`scripts/27_true_fire_mask_and_carbon.py`) found the burned pixels form **540,812 disconnected patches**. The two largest (47,774 ha + 7,666 ha = 55,440 ha) match the real fire almost exactly; the remaining ~48,000 ha is scattered in hundreds of thousands of tiny patches across Antalya city, roads, and farmland — classification false-positives (cloud shadow, harvest, construction), **not fire**. A user screenshot of the classified raster over a basemap visually confirmed speckled "burn" pixels across urban Antalya, which is not physically plausible as fire.

**Fix:** keep only contiguous regions ≥100 ha (20 patches, 60,128 ha total — matches published figures). Outputs:
- **`dNBR_True_Fire_Only.tif`**: dNBR classified raster, noise removed (all ≥100ha regions from across the 2021 fire season in the AOI).
- **`Main_Manavgat_Fire.tif`** / **`main_fire_perimeter.shp`**: just the single largest contiguous patch (47,774 ha) — the actual Manavgat fire scar, used for all population/buffer/perimeter work (section 4c).

**This noise also affected earlier validation results** (`scripts/21`, `scripts/24`, run on the noisy full mask). Re-run with the true mask (`scripts/28_revalidate_with_true_fire_mask.py`, see `manavgat_dogrulama_temiz_maske.png`) **confirmed the same conclusions hold**: aspect still validates cleanly (south 0.436 > east/west 0.409 > north 0.364 mean dNBR), slope still does not (non-monotonic: mid-slope 0.433 > steep 0.395 > flat 0.379), NDVI-severity relationship is still cleanly monotonic (0.47 → 0.56 → 0.60 → 0.69). No changes needed to the V3 risk model as a result.

### 4c. Carbon Emission Estimate (approximate, not field-measured — but now uses real satellite biomass)
`scripts/27_true_fire_mask_and_carbon.py` first estimated CO2 using the IPCC 2006 AFOLU method (Eq. 2.14: Area × Biomass × Combustion Factor × Emission Factor) with biomass as a generic low/mid/high literature guess (50/90/130 t/ha) for Mediterranean pine forest — a "Tier 1" approach. This was superseded by `scripts/31_carbon_with_real_biomass.py`, which replaces the guessed biomass with **ESA CCI Biomass v6.0 (2020, 100m, satellite-derived, downloaded from the CEDA anonymous FTP archive)** — actual pixel-level biomass density for this specific forest, for the year before the fire. Combustion factor is still severity-scaled and literature-based (not directly measured — this part is unavoidably an estimate).

**Corrected result: ~2.97 million tonnes CO2** (range 2.38M–3.56M, using ESA CCI Biomass's own documented ±20% relative-error spec, not an arbitrary guess) — roughly equivalent to ~646,000 cars' annual emissions. Real measured biomass in the burn scar (50–64 t/ha) turned out closer to the earlier "low" literature scenario than the "mid" one, so **the earlier 4.40M tonne estimate was too high** — this correction is a genuine improvement, not just a narrower range. See `manavgat_karbon_emisyonu.png` (now reflects the corrected figure). **Still present as "tahmini" (estimated) in the StoryMap** — real biomass input, but combustion completeness is still inferred, not measured.

### 4d. Real Population/Settlement Impact Analysis
The original `scripts/08_buffer_analysis.py` used a **fabricated 8km circle** around a single point (not the real fire shape) and **placeholder/example village coordinates** (literally commented "Örnek Manavgat köyleri koordinatları" in the code) — not real data. Replaced by `scripts/29a_main_fire_perimeter.py` + `scripts/29_population_impact_analysis.py`, which use:
- The real fire perimeter (`main_fire_perimeter.shp`, from 4b).
- Real settlement locations from OpenStreetMap Overpass API (175 named places, `overpass_settlements.json`).
- Real distances (`arcpy.analysis.Near` in UTM 36N).
- Real population counts from **WorldPop 2020** (UN-adjusted, constrained, 100m, `tur_population_2020.tif`), zonal-summed within 0-1km / 1-3km / 3-5km rings built from the true perimeter.

**Result: ~134,676 people within 5km of the fire** (1,623 inside the burn scar itself, 9,116 within 1km, 43,774 within 1-3km, 80,162 within 3-5km) — 46 named settlements within 1km, 29 within 1-3km, 18 within 3-5km. See updated `manavgat_yerlesim_buffer_analizi.png`. Note: the point labels are cluttered in the dense southern cluster near Manavgat town — consider a cropped inset for the StoryMap.

### Model Validation Outputs
- **`manavgat_risk_model_dogrulama.png`**: Bar charts of mean real dNBR by slope class and by aspect class (the validation described above).
- **`manavgat_ndvi_siddet_iliskisi.png`**: Mean pre-fire NDVI by real burn severity class.
- **`manavgat_ruzgar_kuraklik_analizi.png`**: Cumulative 2021 precipitation and dominant wind direction during the fire (28 Jul–10 Aug), overlaid on the dNBR map. **Note on wording:** the 2021 fire-season anomaly vs. the 2015–2020 baseline was mostly a **temperature** anomaly (+1.8°C), not a precipitation/drought anomaly (-0.8mm, negligible). Avoid calling this a "drought" in the StoryMap — "anomalously hot, typically-dry Mediterranean summer" is the accurate framing.

## 5. Script Workflow Summaries
The python scripts written for this project automate the entire pipeline (found in the `scripts/` directory):
- **Scripts 10 to 14**: Handle STAC API querying, bounding box intersection, and batch downloading of specific bands (B08, B12, TCI) across multiple years and tiles.
- **Scripts 15 to 19**: Handle the GIS operations via `arcpy`. They mosaic the North and South tiles, calculate NBR, mask the results to the exact fire extent to avoid edge-artifacts, and assign proper coordinate systems.
- **Script 20**: A utility cleanup script that deletes intermediate temp files, corrupted downloads, and unneeded raw bands to save disk space and maintain a clean workspace.
